Concept for a norns script · grid 128 · arc 4

monsun

A 16-tap rhythm delay and a 64-tooth comb resonator, modelled on the Intellijel / Cylonix Rainmaker. The grid is a tap editor: its 16 columns are the 16 taps. The arc sculpts the time and the space; norns shows the rhythm and the comb.

16 rhythm taps 16 grooves 64 comb teeth 16 comb patterns 4 routings 4 comb feedback types freeze · reverse · clear · ping clock sync

Working name: monsun, the monsoon. This page is the plan, not the finished script: try the timing and comb views below, then tell me what to change before I build it.

Signal flow

Rhythm delay

One 20-second buffer, read by 16 taps. Each tap has its own pitch shifter (±16 semitones, granular), a resonant filter (low, band or high pass), level and pan. A feedback path takes one tap, all taps, or the comb size, shifts its pitch, colours it and writes it back.

Comb resonator

A second buffer read by 64 teeth whose spacing follows one of 16 patterns. Its size sets the pitch, so a ping turns it into a plucked string (Karplus-Strong). The feedback loop is clean (raw), damped (guitar), buzzing (sitar) or reedy (clarinet).

Routing

Delay into comb (default), comb into delay, both in parallel, or split: left input to the delay, right input to the comb. The dry signal always reaches the mix.

Where the 16 taps land

Tap times come from three settings: the beat (from the norns clock, so MIDI clock works), the grid (taps per beat) and the groove. Groove amount fades from straight to the chosen pattern. A stack gives several taps the same time, which makes chords when their pitches differ. Try it:

The comb's 64 teeth

Each tooth is a tap into the comb buffer. The pattern spaces them across the comb size, density sets how many sound, and slope shapes their levels. At short sizes this is timbre; at long sizes it becomes swells, crescendos and clouds.

Grid 128 · four pages

Row 8 is the same on every page: page keys on the left, then six keys that change with the page, then RAMP and RANDOM, then the performance keys PING, FREEZE, REVERSE and ALT. Hover or tap a key to read what it does.

Arc 4 follows the grid page

Hold ALT for fine adjustment on every ring. Rings light from 7 o'clock to 5 o'clock; tap select shows 16 segments; bipolar values grow from 12 o'clock.

norns screen · E1 pages

E2 selects a value, E3 changes it. K2 freeze, K3 ping. K1 is left to the norns menu.

Engine plan and differences from the module

Built as on the module

  • 16 taps with level, pan, filter type, cutoff, Q, pitch and detune each
  • 16 grooves with amount, grid 1–16 taps per beat, tap stacks
  • Feedback from any tap, all taps or the comb size, with slip, pitch, tone and ping-pong
  • Freeze, reverse and clear on the delay buffer
  • 64-tooth comb, 16 spacing patterns, density, 4 slopes, 4 feedback types
  • Delay-time LFO and comb-size LFO, 8 waveforms each
  • 4 routings, ramps across taps, randomize by destination

Adapted for norns

  • The 16 taps read a mono sum of the input and are panned one by one. That halves the CPU and still gives a wide stereo picture. The dry signal stays stereo.
  • Pitch shifting uses two crossfading grains per tap with a selectable grain size (the module offers 1, 2 or 4 grains).
  • The comb feedback runs through norns' 64-sample control blocks. Pitched combs with all four feedback types reach up to about F5; above that a simpler sample-accurate comb takes over.
  • No CV inputs. In their place: norns MAP for MIDI CC on every parameter, and the arc.
  • Presets morph: recalling one glides between the tap settings.

How I'll test it before you get it

  • Render the engine offline in SuperCollider and measure tap times, pitch shifts, filter responses, comb pitch accuracy and feedback stability
  • Run the script with the real norns libraries: grid pages, arc, screen, presets and clock
  • Measure CPU against the reverb we just built
  • An audio demo of the presets on test sounds

Decisions before I build

Mono taps, panned (planned), or fully stereo taps?

Stereo taps keep the left and right inputs separate in every echo, at roughly twice the CPU. On the original norns that could be too heavy.

Play the comb from MIDI notes?

The COMB page already has a 4-row keyboard. A MIDI note input would let a keyboard or sequencer play the comb as a plucked voice: note sets pitch, velocity sets ping strength.

Presets: 96 slots with morph, and an A↔B crossfade on the arc?

The PRESETS page holds 96 slots (the first 12 factory presets). Ring 1 there blends between two chosen presets, so you can sweep between two rhythms live.

A browser preview?

I can also port the engine to the web simulator like dekade, so you can play it before copying it to norns. That adds time; the norns folder comes first either way.
